The habits behind the settings

Safety is a practice, not a promise.

A few steady habits can make your digital life feel less noisy and more yours. Start small, understand the trade-offs, and ask for help when the stakes are high.

01 / focus + notifications

Protect your attention deliberately.

Use Focus or Do Not Disturb to choose what can interrupt you. Begin with a short study window, allow essential calls, and check the exceptions so the mode behaves the way you expect.

  • Try a 25–45 minute focus block.
  • Review repeated-call and emergency exceptions.
  • Tell close contacts when you are taking quiet time.
02 / evenings + sleep

Make the last hour predictable.

Use a bedtime schedule, lower stimulation, or simply charge your phone away from the bed. The most sustainable routine is the one you can repeat without a fight.

  • Set a wind-down reminder you can keep.
  • Reduce non-essential notifications before bed.
  • Keep alarms and important contacts available.
03 / tracking awareness

Notice what a permission means.

Browser extensions can read or change website content because that is how they do their job. Install from the official store, check the publisher, and give only the access you understand.

  • Read the permission warning before installing.
  • Keep an eye on browser compatibility.
  • Disable an extension for a single site if needed.
04 / accounts + passwords

Make reuse harder.

Use a different password for important accounts, turn on multi-factor authentication where available, and keep a recovery method you can access.

  • Start with email, banking, and college accounts.
  • Never share a password or one-time code.
  • Pause when a message creates urgency.
A simple pause

Before you click, install, or share.

Use this quick check when something feels urgent or unusually convenient.

ASK 01

Who is asking?

Check the sender, publisher, domain, and context. Urgency is not proof.

ASK 02

What access is needed?

A useful feature may need access, but you should understand why and whether it can be revoked.

ASK 03

What happens next?

Know how to undo the setting, update the password, or report the message before you proceed.

Important noteThis hub is an educational resource, not medical, mental-health, legal, or cybersecurity emergency advice. If you are worried about immediate safety, harassment, fraud, or account compromise, contact a trusted person or the appropriate official support service.
